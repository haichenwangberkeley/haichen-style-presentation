# Analysis updates and comparisons

Read this reference for a working-meeting update, a comparison between methods or trainings, or any result presented as a change from an earlier baseline. The purpose is to make the differential answerable without requiring the audience to reconstruct the earlier result.

## Start from the analysis record

Locate, when available:

- the latest complete analysis presentation or equivalent record;
- the previous result used as the comparison baseline;
- the current result workspace and its version;
- the prepared analysis-question checklist and recorded answers.

Select one authoritative current snapshot before drafting: the completion or summary artifact, workspace or release identity, configuration, and active omission or exception ledger. If multiple outputs disagree, resolve which one controls the headline result before using their numbers. Do not silently take status from one release, limits from another, and failures from a third.

Keep the complete presentation as the durable source of analysis definitions, samples, selections, inputs, and established results. Derive the short meeting update from that source and the current workspace. Do not create a disconnected short deck whose definitions drift from the complete record.

## Isolate the differential

State the baseline and list every material change, including changes to:

- data or simulated samples;
- signal and background definitions;
- object selections, working points, or categories;
- training samples, inputs, architecture, or optimization;
- algorithms, corrections, fit models, or systematic treatment;
- normalization, statistical procedure, or figure of merit;
- interpretation or analysis status.

Prefer a comparison in which one factor changes. When several factors changed, list them on the comparison slide and limit the conclusion accordingly.

## Check comparability

Before presenting an improvement, ratio, or difference, verify that the compared quantities use compatible definitions, samples, selections, working points, categories, normalization, statistical treatment, and metrics. Check that the statement on the slide agrees with the plotted or tabulated change.

If the cases are not directly comparable, state why and do not present the observed difference as the effect of one method. Show the mismatched conditions or replace the comparison with one that answers a well-defined question.

## Show a quantitative comparison

Keep the baseline visible. Prefer a ratio, difference, residual, efficiency change, or another directly interpretable differential over two unconnected panels. State what changed and what the comparison establishes.

When there is no new result, do not fill the slide with unanchored numbers. State the present interpretation, limitation, decision, or next check.

For a compact working-meeting update, use this structure when appropriate:

1. purpose and main message;
2. baseline and changes;
3. minimum method or workflow needed to understand the change;
4. quantitative differential;
5. interpretation and limitations;
6. open question or next action.

## Select evidence by scientific function

- Use plots for shapes, trends, and physical behavior.
- Use tables for definitions, coverage, and exact numerical values.
- Show both when exact values and shape information are independently necessary.
- For a Higgs result, retain the relevant mass distributions rather than replacing them with a yield or significance table alone.
- For an apparent signal or localized feature, show the templates, uncertainties, or other evidence needed to assess whether the behavior is compatible with fluctuation or modeling.
- Retain uncertainty columns and comparison cases when they change the interpretation.

Use consistent chart conventions for repeated category or selection diagrams. Replace opaque internal run names with descriptive labels that identify the training, sample, method, or configuration being compared.

## Preserve traceability

Every claim should be traceable to an inspectable result, workspace, or checklist answer. Keep a private companion record of workspace pointers, comparison inputs, source versions, active omissions or exceptions, and unresolved questions. Do not expose private paths or internal production details on public-facing slides unless requested.

## Acceptance

An update is ready when the audience can determine, without consulting an earlier deck:

- what the reference result is;
- everything that changed;
- whether the compared cases are genuinely comparable;
- the quantitative size and direction of the difference;
- which visual or numerical evidence supports the interpretation;
- what remains unresolved and what happens next.
